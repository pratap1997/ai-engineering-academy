# Real-World Applications of VLMs

## 1. IBM Docling & Document Parsing
IBM's Docling leverages vision-language concepts to parse complex PDFs, research papers, and documents. Traditional text extractors fail on tables, charts, and equations because they lose structural layout. By treating the document as an image and processing it via a Vision-Language architecture, the model visually "sees" the table grid while reading the text, allowing for perfect Markdown/JSON extraction of complex formats.

## 2. Autonomous Driving Perception
Companies like Tesla and Wayve use multimodal architectures to combine camera feeds (vision tokens) with navigational goals and rules (text tokens). The Vision Encoder processes the real-time road conditions, while the LLM backbone reasons about safety rules and path planning, outputting steering and acceleration commands based on joint understanding.

## 3. LLaVA (Large Language-and-Vision Assistant)
LLaVA is an open-source pioneer in VLMs. It connects a pre-trained CLIP ViT-L/14 visual encoder to the LLaMA language model. It was trained on synthetic datasets generated by GPT-4, allowing users to upload images and chat with the model about the contents seamlessly, demonstrating strong zero-shot performance on Visual Question Answering (VQA).

## 4. GPT-4o Omni-Modal Capabilities
OpenAI's GPT-4o natively integrates text, vision, and audio without separate disconnected models. Its vision-language pipeline allows users to show the model a live video feed from their phone camera. It can instantly recognize coding errors on a screen, solve handwritten math equations, or describe the emotional state of a person based on facial expressions—all in real-time.
